1. Introduction

    Human society is increasingly grappling with the devastating impacts of environmental degradation. Unsustainable industrial
practices and excessive consumption are fueling air pollution, poisoning our waters, and disrupting ecosystems, all of which threaten
biodiversity and the well-being of public health. The shipping industry stands as a major contributor in this crisis, heavily relying on
fossil fuels, and producing nearly 3% of global CO2 emissions, more than the whole emissions of advanced economies like Germany
or Japan (IMO, 2023). Without decisive action, emissions from this sector could soar by as much as 50% by 2050, highlighting the
urgent need for low-carbon technologies in maritime transport to ensure a sustainable future.
    As the maritime sector faces mounting pressure to mitigate its environmental externalities, electriﬁcation has emerged as a pivotal
path toward decarbonization. By replacing fossil-fuel propulsion, electric vessels eliminate direct emissions of CO2 , NO𝑥 , and SO𝑥 ,
while also cutting underwater noise pollution by 40–50 dB, an essential improvement for the protection of marine ecosystems (DNV,
2024). Policy frameworks are accelerating this transformation, with the IMO’s 2023 GHG Strategy mandating that 90% of major

ports will be shore-power compatible by 2030 (IMO, 2023), and the UN-endorsed Clydebank Declaration establishing zero-emission
corridors to prioritize electric ships across 24 nations (Department for Transport, 2021). As a result, maritime electriﬁcation has
progressed from experimental pilots to commercially viable deployments, with all-electric ships (AESs) increasingly becoming a
mainstream solution for reducing the environmental footprint.
    Building on the growing adoption of AESs in green ports, Ship-to-Grid (S2G) technology oﬀers a compelling opportunity to further
enhance energy eﬃciency and accelerate the integration of renewable energy. By enabling bidirectional energy ﬂow between berthed
AESs and the port’s power grid, S2G turns AESs into mobile energy storage units, allowing them to not only draw power when needed
but also feed surplus energy back into the grid, fostering a dynamic and sustainable energy ecosystem. S2G brings substantial beneﬁts
to both ports and shipping companies. For ports, it facilitates ﬂexible freighting management, helping stabilize the power grid while
also providing a reliable backup power source during emergencies. For shipping companies, it opens up new revenue streams by
enabling the sale of surplus energy back to the grid. As a result, government policy initiatives are accelerating the adoption of S2G,
with examples such as the EU’s 2030 infrastructure mandate (EU, 2021) and China’s ﬁnancial incentives for retroﬁts (MOT, 2024).
These initiatives highlight S2G’s potential to strengthen port resilience and contribute to the goal of maritime decarbonization.
    The integration of S2G technology substantially increases the complexity of the conventional ship scheduling problem. In addition
to planning freight transportation and vessel relocation, shipping operators must also coordinate AESs’ charging and discharging
operations to eﬀectively interact with the port’s power grid. For example, during periods of high electricity demand, AESs can
discharge stored energy back into the grid to stabilize grid performance and enhance economic returns, whereas in oﬀ-peak periods,
they may prioritize recharging to ensure adequate power for subsequent voyages. Within this framework, AESs assume a dual role, as
both transportation carriers and mobile energy storage units capable of storing and supplying electricity. This dual role fundamentally
reshapes the conventional ship scheduling paradigm and highlights the need for more advanced and integrated decision-making
frameworks.
    Another critical challenge in AESs scheduling arises from the uncertainty of freight demand. Unlike conventional ships that
serve broad and relatively stable cargo streams, where freight demand is typically treated as static and stable, AESs concentrate on a
narrower range of time-sensitive and high-value commodities, such as e-commerce parcels, medical supplies, and perishables (Ivanov,
2022). Accordingly, freight demand of the AESs scheduling model is heavily inﬂuenced by contextual information such as weather
conditions, and hinterland characteristics, rendering it inherently context-dependent. For example, ﬂuctuations in weather conditions,
such as extreme temperatures or storms, can impact the operating conditions and service availability of AESs, thereby aﬀecting freight
demand. Meanwhile, the characteristics of the hinterland, including industrial structure, regional economic activity, and short-term
consumption patterns, can also directly inﬂuence demand. Failing to account for such contextual information prevents the model from
accurately capturing freight demand, which may lead to biased forecasts and suboptimal scheduling outcomes. Therefore, addressing
the AESs scheduling problem necessitates the integration of contextual information to better predict uncertain freight demand and
generate more reliable and eﬀective operational decisions.
    In this study, we introduce a novel dynamic AESs scheduling problem by integrating S2G technology, explicitly considering the
context-dependent uncertainty in freight demand. To the best of our knowledge, this is the ﬁrst attempt to explore the AESs operations
within the S2G framework. We focus on the following two research questions: (i) How can we formulate an optimization model for
the dynamic AESs scheduling problem that simultaneously addresses decision-making for AESs chartering, freighting, relocating,
and charging/discharging operations? (ii) How can we construct a reliable uncertainty set for context-dependent uncertainty while
maintaining model tractability and optimality? By solving these research questions, we summarize our contributions as follows.



(1) Enriching the ship scheduling problem. After constructing spatial-temporal-SoC network, we propose a dynamic AESs schedul-
    ing model for shipping company to produce operational scheduling scheme, which introduces the following new aspects:
     • We incorporate S2G operations into the dynamic AESs scheduling problem, meaning that beyond freight routing and AESs

       relocation, the model must also coordinate AESs’ charging and discharging activities to interact eﬀectively with the port’s
       power grid.
     • We explicitly account for context-dependent uncertainty in AES freight demand, whose variation can be partially explained

       by observable contextual information such as weather conditions and port throughput.
(2) Extending the existing methodology. We propose a contextual joint estimation-prediction-optimization (JEPO) framework,
    which advances the methodology in the following aspects:
     • Beyond the classical norm-based metric, we propose a contextual disparity metric that embeds a deviation matrix, thereby

       allowing deviations to be evaluated in a more principled manner that reﬂects dimensional heterogeneity.
     • In contrast to the sequential process of estimation, prediction, and optimization, the proposed JEPO framework oﬀers a uniﬁed

       data-to-decision paradigm by embedding the estimation and prediction procedures directly into the uncertainty set of the
       optimization model.
     • We propose a data-driven calibration approach for the JEPO uncertainty set by identifying the minimum volume conﬁdence

       ball without compromising the conﬁdence level, thereby mitigating the conservatism of the robust solutions.
     • Using a linear decision rule approximation and a lifted uncertainty set construction, we demonstrate that the contextual JEPO

       model admits a tractable second-order cone program (SOCP) formulation.
(3) Demonstrating superior performance in numerical experiments. We validate the practical eﬀectiveness of our approach using

2. Literature review

   In this section, we review the related literature. Speciﬁcally, Sections 2.1 and 2.2 examine the literature on the standard ship
scheduling problem and maritime electriﬁcation, respectively, while Section 2.3 provides an overview of methodological studies on
optimization under uncertainty.

2.1. Standard ship scheduling problem

    In maritime logistics, ship scheduling plays an indispensable and pivotal role, involving the optimization of ﬂeet deployment and
freight routing under multiple sources of uncertainty. Early works, such as Christiansen et al. (2004), provide a systematic review of
ship routing and scheduling problems across strategic, tactical, and operational levels. They separately discuss two types of shipping
operations: tramp and liner shipping, which exhibit distinct diﬀerences in their scheduling strategies. Speciﬁcally, tramp shipping is
characterized by high operational ﬂexibility. Vessels are deployed according to cargo availability rather than ﬁxed routes or schedules,
allowing shipping companies to maximize proﬁts by dynamically optimizing scheduling decisions. In contrast, liner shipping follows
ﬁxed routes and schedules and its operations focusing on strategic network design, ﬂeet sizing, and ﬂeet deployment.
    Tramp shipping scheduling primarily aims to optimize routing and bunkering decisions to maximize proﬁts under uncertain
conditions. Vilhelmsen et al. (2014) examine the integration of bunkering decisions with routing, suggesting that incorporating
bunkering considerations during the routing phase enhances cargo allocation and eﬃciency, particularly in the face of ﬂuctuating fuel
prices. Building on this, Omholt-Jensen et al. (2025) model the tramp ship routing and scheduling problem with bunker optimization
as a two-stage stochastic programming problem. They propose that integrating bunkering decisions with routing can increase proﬁts
by adapting to uncertainties in demand and fuel costs. Additionally, Norstad et al. (2011) explore speed optimization, arguing that
treating speed as a decision variable rather than a ﬁxed parameter which improves operational eﬃciency by more eﬀectively managing
fuel consumption. Wen et al. (2016) extend this concept by jointly optimizing routing and speed, suggesting that this combined
approach enhances ﬂeet proﬁtability and overall operational performance.
    Liner shipping scheduling is fundamental to ensuring service quality and operational eﬃciency. Early research primarily employs
deterministic models with ﬁxed sailing speeds, port times, and demand, aiming to optimize costs or proﬁts (Meng et al., 2010; Qi
et al., 2012). However, such models often neglect operational uncertainties, including port congestion, handling delays, and adverse
weather conditions. More recent studies incorporate stochastic elements into scheduling frameworks by applying fuzzy logic, chance-
constrained programming, and simulation-based optimization methods (Chuang et al., 2010; Wang et al., 2012). To enhance schedule
reliability in the face of disruptions, researchers increasingly examine recovery strategies such as speed adjustments, port skipping,
and container diversion (Song et al., 2015; Gürel et al., 2019).
    While existing tramp and liner scheduling studies focus on conventional fuel-powered ships, the scheduling of AESs remains
underexplored. The distinct operational features of AESs, particularly the need to coordinate charging/discharging operations with
cargo freighting and ship relocating, call for specialized AESs scheduling strategies. To bridge this gap, we conceptualize a dynamic
AESs scheduling model in the context of S2G, which explicitly accounts for the context-dependent uncertainty in freight demand.

2.2. Maritime electriﬁcation

   This study is also related to maritime electriﬁcation, which plays a vital role in reducing emissions and enhancing energy eﬃciency
within the shipping industry. The adoption of S2G technology has transformed the energy management of conventional vessels,
thereby inﬂuencing the optimization and scheduling of AESs. To lay the foundation for addressing the AES scheduling problem, this
section focuses on AESs and S2G, which are essential components in advancing maritime electriﬁcation.
   Eﬃcient voyage scheduling becomes a crucial factor in optimizing the operations of AESs. Early research by Kanellos et al. (2014)
focuses on coordinating voyage scheduling with power dispatch in hybrid power systems. They develop a dynamic programming
model that simultaneously optimizes AES speed and onboard power generation to reduce overall energy consumption. Recognizing
that speed optimization alone may limit operational ﬂexibility, Wen et al. (2020) extend this work by introducing a joint optimization
approach that integrates voyage planning with the sizing of onboard energy storage systems, aiming to enhance both economic

eﬃciency and renewable energy utilization. As scheduling objectives become increasingly complex, Shang et al. (2016) propose a
bi-objective model that considers both operational costs and greenhouse gas emissions, solved using NSGA-II to derive Pareto-optimal
scheduling strategies. Building on these advances, Hein et al. (2021) develop a multi-objective framework for voyage planning and
energy management in AESs equipped with hybrid energy storage systems, striving to balance environmental sustainability and cost
eﬃciency under practical maritime operational constraints. However, existing scheduling literature on AESs primarily emphasizes
energy-related aspects, often overlooking the scheduling challenges that arise in shipping networks under uncertain freight demand.
    Simultaneously, the integration of S2G technology has attracted growing attention in the context of AESs. This technology enables
AESs to not only draw power from the grid but also feed power back when required. Although research on S2G is still in its early stages
and existing studies primarily focus on technical aspects (Reed et al., 2012; Vlachokostas et al., 2019; Badakhshan et al., 2022), related
research on Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) operation management provides valuable insights for our work. Peterson et al. (2010) examine the
economic implications of using electric vehicle batteries for grid electricity storage, ﬁnding that while battery degradation impacts
proﬁtability, the potential for energy arbitrage remains signiﬁcant. Zhang et al. (2021) further explore V2G integration in electric
vehicle sharing systems, demonstrating that V2G increases proﬁtability and service quality.
    Although S2G and V2G both rely on bidirectional charging technology, they diﬀer substantially in their operational settings.
In V2G systems, vehicle mobility is determined by individual users, who decide when and where their EVs travel. As a result, the
operator plays a largely passive role and can only predict vehicle availability based on travel behavior and participation patterns.
Accordingly, V2G typically operates in a decentralized manner, where the timing and location of charging and discharging cannot
be directly controlled by the operator. Instead, the operator usually relies on incentive mechanisms, such as ﬁnancial compensa-
tion, to encourage EV owners to participate in charging or discharging activities at desired times. By contrast, S2G systems are
centrally managed by the shipping company, which directly determines the operations of AESs. This centralized structure allows
the operator to actively coordinate freight transportation as well as charging and discharging decisions. In particular, the operator
can jointly optimize routing, freight scheduling, and energy transactions in response to variations in freight demand and electricity
prices. Therefore, unlike V2G, S2G does not rely on voluntary participation through incentives, but instead is implemented through
centralized operational decisions made by the company. However, research on S2G operation management strategies remains limited.
To address this gap, our study proposes a scheduling framework for AESs that integrates S2G technology. The framework aims to
enhance operational eﬃciency and optimize revenue generation, particularly in the context of uncertainties associated with freight
demand.

2.3. Optimization under uncertainty

    Conventionally, uncertainty in optimization problems has been addressed primarily through stochastic programming (SP) and
robust optimization (RO). While both have formed the foundation of uncertainty modeling, they suﬀer from critical limitations.
Speciﬁcally, SP relies on the precise characterization of the probability distribution associated with uncertain parameters–an assump-
tion that is often unrealistic in many practical scenarios. On the other hand, RO completely excludes probabilistic information and
instead focuses on worst-case outcomes, which tends to produce overly cautious decisions. To mitigate these shortcomings, distri-
butionally robust optimization (DRO) has emerged as a compelling alternative (Wang et al., 2020). Despite their theoretical appeal,
constructing such sets using empirical data and domain-speciﬁc knowledge remains a substantial challenge. In response, Long et al.
(2023) introduce the robust satisﬁcing framework, which eliminates the need for specifying a distributional set altogether. More
recently, Sim et al. (2024) further advance this framework by incorporating predictive models and decision-making mechanisms,
enhancing its practicality across diverse uncertain environments.
    Contextual robust optimization has recently attracted signiﬁcant attention for its ability to incorporate covariate information
into decision-making under uncertainty (Mišić and Perakis, 2020). Within this paradigm, three major frameworks have emerged for
learning decision policies from data. The decision rule framework, ﬁrst introduced to operations research by Liyanage and Shanthiku-
mar (2005) and later popularized by Ban and Rudin (2019), directly parameterizes decision policies and optimizes their parameters
through empirical risk minimization. The sequential learning and optimization (SLO) framework, formalized by Bertsimas and Kallus
(2020), follows a two-step procedure: it ﬁrst estimates the conditional distribution of uncertain parameters given covariates, and
then solves the resulting stochastic optimization problem. Acknowledging the potential mismatch between predictive accuracy and
decision quality in SLO, the integrated learning and optimization (ILO) framework jointly trains the predictive model and decision
policy by directly minimizing the downstream decision loss–an idea originating in Bengio (1997) and later revitalized under the
paradigms of decision-focused learning and smart predict-then-optimize (Donti et al., 2017; Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022).
    While these above research focus on learning decision policies, a critical yet often overlooked aspect is how to construct the
uncertainty set that eﬀectively reﬂect the data distribution. Traditional methods typically assume that uncertainty set parame-
ters are known or set through empirical means and covariance. However, they usually lack a systematic way to estimate these
parameters based on the data distribution and the chosen norm, which can lead to inaccurate uncertainty sets, resulting in so-
lutions that may be overly conservative or insuﬃciently stable. One common approach uses asymptotic behavior, such as the
central limit theorem (Bandi and Bertsimas, 2012), which works well with large sample sizes but can be overly conservative in
small samples sizes, especially with skewed or heavy-tailed distributions. To reduce this conservatism, methods like divergence
functionals (Ben-Tal et al., 2013) and hypothesis testing (Bertsimas et al., 2018) have been incorporated. Hong et al. (2021)
propose a statistical framework that learns uncertainty sets using geometric shapes and data-splitting validation. However, these
methods typically require large, high-quality datasets for reliable performance. To address this challenge, Lou et al. (2024) fo-
cus on developing an estimation method when only a limited number of samples are available. Speciﬁcally, they construct norm-

revenue target, while explicitly balancing robustness and conservatism.

3. Dynamic AESs scheduling problem

   In this section, we focus on the dynamic AESs scheduling problem integrated with S2G charging and discharging operations.
Section 3.1 introduces the problem description, followed by Section 3.2, which constructs the time–space–SoC network for dynamic
AESs scheduling. Finally, Section 3.3 presents the corresponding mathematical formulation.

3.1. Problem description

    In the context of maritime electriﬁcation, we consider a shipping company operating a ﬂeet of AESs that provide freight services
across a set of ports denoted by [𝐼] = {1, … , 𝐼}. The planning horizon is divided into 𝑇 discrete time periods, represented by [𝑇 ] =
{1, … , 𝑇 }. At the beginning of the planning horizon, the company determines a strategic decision 𝑥𝑖 ∈ ℝ+ , indicating the number of
AESs to be chartered at port 𝑖 ∈ [𝐼], subject to the overall budget constraint 𝑀.
    In contrast to conventional fuel-powered vessels, the feasible sailing range of AESs depends on their remaining battery capacity,
quantiﬁed by the State of Charge (SoC), which is discretized into 𝐵 levels denoted by [𝐵] = {1, … , 𝐵}. Without loss of generality,
we assume that newly chartered AESs begin the planning horizon fully charged, i.e., with an SoC level of 𝐵 in the ﬁrst period.
Additionally, in the dynamic scheduling of AESs, we focus on the integration of S2G operations, including both battery charging and
discharging. Because electricity prices vary across periods, the shipping company can proﬁt by charging during low-price periods and
discharging during high-price periods. Formally, we denote the unit electricity prices for charging and discharging at period 𝑡 ∈ [𝑇 ]
by 𝑝𝑐𝑡 and 𝑝𝑠𝑡 , respectively.
    Within this problem context, the chartered AESs are used to fulﬁll the uncertain freight demand across the planning horizon.

indicators, rendering it contextual-based uncertain as illustrated in Fig. C.1. Let 𝑑̃𝑖,𝑗,𝑡 denote the uncertain freight demand transported

such as weather conditions and port throughput. Ignoring such contextual information and relying only on unconditional demand
distributions may lead to systematic prediction bias and, consequently, suboptimal scheduling decisions. In the AESs scheduling set-
ting, a context-agnostic model cannot distinguish high-demand scenarios associated with favorable weather or strong port throughput
from low-demand scenarios induced by adverse operating conditions. As a result, it may either over-charter AESs, thereby incurring
unnecessary idle and charging costs, or under-charter AESs, thereby causing unmet freight demand and lost revenue.
    Nevertheless, freight demand across ports is typically unbalanced. Some ports are primarily exporters with large outbound cargo
but little inbound cargo, while others are mainly importers. Consequently, AESs may need to relocate after completing the previous
cargo transportation to meet new freight demand.
    In the context of uncertain freight demand, shipping company operators face the challenge of determining when and where to
transport cargo, charge or discharge batteries of AESs, and relocate to meet new freight demand, with the overall objective of mini-
mizing the total net cost. Unlike conventional ship scheduling problems, on the one hand, our study incorporates additional charging
and discharging decisions; on the other hand, the revenue of the AESs scheduling problem derives from two components which
include cargo transportation revenue and discharging revenue. Due to the complexity of AESs scheduling problem, which involves
three dimensions, time period, port, and SoC level, it requires implementation on a spatial-temporal-SoC network, as introduced in
Section 3.2.

discharging of rate 1 SoC unit/period, i.e., 1 MWh. We also consider the volatility of electricity prices across diﬀerent time periods,

    For diﬀerent budget levels 𝑀 ∈ {10000, 15000, 20000, 25000, 30000}, Fig. 4 presents the out-of-sample performance of the proposed
JEPO model and the three benchmark models with respect to the revenue objective under diﬀerent evaluation metrics. It can be seen
that the JEPO model consistently outperforms all benchmark models across the tested budget settings. This superior performance
stems from its ability to address the key limitations of the benchmark approaches. Speciﬁcally, the deterministic model treats the
predicted demand as the realized demand and therefore ignores demand uncertainty, which leads to poor performance when the
actual demand deviates from the prediction. The SAA model approximates the realized demand by combining the prediction with
empirical residual samples, but this approximation does not adequately capture the structure of contextual uncertainty, resulting in
inferior out-of-sample performance. The CRO model relies on a standard box-type uncertainty set and does not explicitly account for
the conﬁdence level or control the conservatism of the uncertainty set, which also limits its eﬀectiveness.
    The SRO model diﬀers from CRO by using a diagonal deviation matrix 𝐇 = 𝚲 to perform component-wise standardization of
demand deviations, and indeed outperforms CRO in most budget settings. However, the diagonal structure of 𝚲 captures only the
standard deviation of each demand component separately, while ignoring the cross-dimensional correlations among ports and time
periods. By contrast, the JEPO model constructs the uncertainty set by explicitly incorporating both estimation and prediction pro-
cedures, and estimates a full matrix 𝐇∗ via the MVCB approach to jointly capture demand heterogeneity and cross-dimensional
correlations. This eﬀectively reduces the excessive conservatism of robust decisions and thereby improves out-of-sample perfor-
mance.

level 𝑍0 (𝑀, 𝑣). Speciﬁcally, we consider 𝜏 = 0.85𝑍0 (𝑀, 𝑣) and 𝜏 = 1.15𝑍0 (𝑀, 𝑣) and evaluate the corresponding 95% CVaR out-of-

both target settings.

𝑀 = 10000. Compared with the benchmark models, the JEPO model tends to derive a mild allocation plan, i.e., it does not allocate
too many AESs in some speciﬁc ports and thereby results in a stable revenue.
    We also report the computational times of the JEPO model and the three benchmark models under diﬀerent budget levels 𝑀 in

Nevertheless, its runtime increases only moderately, from 1764 to 2032 seconds, as the budget level increases. This indicates that
the JEPO model maintains stable computational performance across diﬀerent budget settings. Moreover, since the AES scheduling
problem considered here is a strategic planning problem rather than a real-time operational problem, the observed computational
time remains acceptable for practical implementation.

6.4. Case study

    In this subsection, we present a case study involving eight representative ports in the North Sea region, Europe, analyzed using
our proposed model. These ports include major international hubs such as Rotterdam, Antwerp, and Vlissingen, as well as smaller
regional ports, including Breda and Haarlem. Fig. 8 illustrates the average freight demand for each port.
    To evaluate the economic beneﬁts under varying S2G levels of adoption, this study performs a sensitivity analysis on the S2G
penetration rate within the eight ports under budget 𝐵 = 100000. Here, the S2G penetration rate refers to the proportion of AESs that
are capable of bidirectional power exchange with the grid. Five scenarios are considered, corresponding to 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and
100% S2G-equipped AESs. All other model parameters, including port electricity prices, charter budget, and freight demand, remain
constant to ensure comparability.
    Fig. 9 demonstrates that there is a clear upward trend in total revenue as the S2G penetration rate increases. The baseline sce-
nario (0%) yields the lowest revenue, as AESs are unable to discharg electricity to the grid. At 25% penetration, revenue rises by
approximately 17.95%. The 50% and 75% scenarios continue this upward trajectory, with proﬁt increases of 14.93% and 15.21%,
respectively. However, beyond 75% penetration, marginal gains begin to taper oﬀ. Speciﬁcally, the 100% scenario provides only
a 5.06% improvement compared to 75%. This deceleration in growth suggests that the system is approaching saturation in S2G
utilization, as most ports are already equipped with suﬃcient AESs to discharge electricity back to the power grid.
    Fig. 10 presents the eight selected ports (marked as blue) which are equipped with S2G technology. Using the freight demand data
among these ports, we apply our model to obtain the distribution and routing of AESs across the North Sea region, Europe. Based on
this analysis, we derive the following management insights.
    First, the three major hub ports, Rotterdam, Antwerp, and Hamburg, account for over 65% of AESs, as they handle a larger volume
of shipping operations. Government should prioritize the deployment of fast-charging/battery-swapping facilities in hub ports and
then use feeder services to extend coverage to regional ports like Haarlem.

7. Conclusions

    Shipping companies face mounting pressure from increasingly stringent environmental regulations and rising fuel costs, making
AESs and S2G technology central to the sector’s operational transformation. This study develops a dynamic AES scheduling model on
a spatial-temporal-SoC network that captures ﬁve key operational behaviors: freight routing, vessel relocation, idle docking, charging,
and discharging. We explicitly consider context-dependent uncertainty in AES freight demand, whose ﬂuctuations can be partially
inferred from contextual information such as seasonality and hinterland economic indicators. Methodologically, we propose a contex-
tual joint estimation–prediction–optimization (JEPO) framework that establishes a uniﬁed data-to-decision paradigm by embedding
estimation and prediction procedures directly into the optimization model’s uncertainty set. To reduce excessive conservatism, we
further develop a data-driven calibration approach that identiﬁes a minimum-volume conﬁdence ball while preserving the desired
conﬁdence level. From a computational perspective, the model is reformulated using linear decision rules and a lifted uncertainty set
construction, and a binary-search algorithm with adaptive intervals is employed to ensure convergence to optimal solutions.
    We validate the proposed approach using real-world instances from ports in the North Sea region of Europe. The results show that
the JEPO model consistently outperforms benchmark models under both average and risk-averse criteria. In addition, comparative
experiments indicate that incorporating S2G operations yields further revenue gains, particularly during periods of pronounced elec-
tricity price volatility. Sensitivity analyses on S2G penetration further reveal a nonmonotonic eﬀect, with marginal revenue increasing
initially and then diminishing as S2G penetration rises. Our ﬁndings oﬀer several managerial insights. For shipping companies, prof-
itability can be improved by strategically deploying AESs equipped with S2G capabilities in response to seasonal electricity price
variations. At higher levels of S2G penetration, however, ﬁrms must carefully balance marginal revenue gains against associated
investment and operational costs to maximize overall returns. From a policy perspective, our results highlight the importance of pri-
oritizing battery-swapping infrastructure in major ports and developing segmented electriﬁcation routes supported by oﬀshore charging
facilities to address the challenges of long-haul AES operations.
